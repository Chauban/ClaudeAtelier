## 背景

*（不印在卡面上，只用来帮渲染决定构图与意象。）*

Bismuth was, for decades, the periodic table's poster child of stability — the heaviest element thought to be non-radioactive — until 2003, when a team at the Institut d'astrophysique spatiale in Orsay, France, caught it decaying. The catch came as a by-product of a dark-matter search: they cooled a 'scintillating bolometer' to 20 mK (near absolute zero) and, watching tens of grams of bismuth for 5 days, counted just 128 alpha-particle events. That works out to a half-life of roughly 2 × 10¹⁹ years — over a billion times the age of the universe — so a lump of bismuth is less radioactive than human flesh.
