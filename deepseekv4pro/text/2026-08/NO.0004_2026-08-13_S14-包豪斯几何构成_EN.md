## 冷知识

In Mozambique, Yao honey-hunters call greater honeyguides with a loud trill-and-grunt ("brrr-hm"), and the birds lead them to wild bees' nests. In one experiment, that call more than tripled the hunters' chances of finding a hive; after taking the honey, the hunters leave beeswax for the birds, which eat it.（在莫桑比克，尧族采蜜人用一声响亮的“brrr-hm”颤音加喉音呼唤响蜜鴷，这种鸟会带他们找到野蜂巢；一项实验中，这声呼唤让找到蜂巢的概率变为原来的三倍以上，采完蜜后猎人会留下蜂蜡给鸟吃。）

## 背景

*（不印在卡面上，只用来帮渲染决定构图与意象。）*

The guiding bird is small—about 20 cm and 50 g—with bold white tail patches, and it attracts attention with chattering "tya" notes before flying tree to tree toward a bees' nest. In the study, 73 of 97 honeyguide-led hunts found at least one bees' nest, and the "brrr-hm" call more than tripled the odds of a find during 15-minute searches. The reward is dangerous for the bird—angry bees can and do sting honeyguides to death—so the hunters leave beeswax on beds of leaves for it.
