Some things learn to fly only because sinking was never an option.

The sea butterfly is a snail. Limacina helicina, about three millimetres wide, does not swim — it flies. It beats the two lobes of its foot through the same figure-of-eight that carries a fruit fly through air, claps them together and flings them apart for extra lift, and rolls its whole shell up to 60 degrees with every stroke. High-speed cameras confirmed it in 2016: the snail and the fly had arrived at the same solution.
