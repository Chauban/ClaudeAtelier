The fussiness no one thanked him for is the only part that outlived him.

Around 1175 an English canon named Orm wrote some 20,000 lines of verse in a spelling he invented himself: double the consonant, and the vowel before it is short. He did it so priests reading aloud would not mispronounce the words. The one manuscript that survives is in his own hand, thick with his own erasures. No one else took up his system — and it is now a chief source for how English actually sounded in the twelfth century.
